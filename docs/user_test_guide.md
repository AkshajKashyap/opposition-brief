# 15-minute Opposition Brief usability test

## Setup

Before the session, run `opposition-brief build-demo-report --offline` and
`streamlit run app/streamlit_app.py`. Ask a coach, analyst, or video coordinator
to think aloud. Do not explain the answer or defend the product while they work.

## Tasks

1. **Minute 0–3 — Orient:** Ask: “Which candidate observation looks most
   important to review before this opponent?” Record whether they understand
   the factual claim, its sample, and evidence-strength label.
2. **Minute 3–6 — Inspect support:** Ask them to open its evidence explorer,
   find the match/period/timestamp for one supporting event, and place it on
   the pitch. Record whether they can find source evidence without help.
3. **Minute 6–8 — Inspect comparison:** Ask them to explain what the
   counterexample/comparison table does and does not mean. Record whether they
   understand the limitation.
4. **Minute 8–12 — Decide:** Ask them to accept, reject, or mark the finding
   needs revision; then edit the interpretation and add a note for staff. Ask
   whether it could affect their video-review plan.
5. **Minute 12–15 — Export:** Ask them to open Report Review, confirm which
   observations appear, and download the reviewed HTML report.

## Record after each session

- Did they understand the factual claim and its limitation?
- Could they locate source evidence and a timestamp?
- Could the observation change what they review on video?
- What was confusing or slow?
- What did they expect to see but did not see?

Avoid asking only whether they liked the product. Look for decisions they
could make, evidence they trusted or distrusted, and terminology that needs
changing before a wider trial.
